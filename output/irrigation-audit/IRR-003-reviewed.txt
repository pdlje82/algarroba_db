Traditional reforestation
Presentation
Traditional reforestation is a reforestation 
method used as a control group in the Life Nie­
blas project, making it possible to establish a 
database for comparing the results obtained 
in the field with other reforestation method­
ologies. This approach involves planting in­
dividual plants in holes measuring 40x40x40 
cm, without any additional tillage. A minimum 
distance of 1.50 metres is maintained between 
each plant to encourage their growth and de­
velopment. Irrigation is provided by supplying 
water to each plant using PE pipes.
One of the main features of traditional re­
forestation is that no additional equipment is 
required for its implementation. This method is 
widely considered to be suitable for reforest­
ing degraded soils, despite its role as a control 
method in the project. It allows the effects of 
different reforestation approaches to be as­
sessed and conclusions drawn about their re­
spective effectiveness.
Location
Due to the nature of the soil, these measures must be adapted to the peculiarities of each area. 
For example, there are areas where the soil is poor or very stony, which avoids the standard 40 
cm deep planting hole.
Amount of water
Traditional reforestation requires regular irrigation in the first year to ensure the survival and 
growth of the plants. Irrigation water is delivered individually to the base of each plant using PE 
pipes. The amount of water required will depend on the specifics of the site and the water re­
quirements of the plant species selected. It is essential to keep a close eye on soil moisture and 
to provide an adequate amount of water to promote healthy growth.

Autonomous Fluid Discharge System
Presentation and general system description
The Autonomous Fluid Discharge System (AFDS) aims to grow plants that are difficult to access 
autonomously, without energy or human intervention. It was invented from the original idea of a 
farmer called Emilio Navarro Rosario. He has worked on the project collaborating in the develop­
ment and monitoring of these systems together with ITC staff. As a farmer in remote places, with 
difficult access and scarcity of water, Emilio had to overcome the difficulties of the environment 
by making time compatible with other jobs. This intelligent solution has not only saved you time, 
but has also given you autonomy and a considerable reduction in energy use.
The AFDS reforestation systems will be aimed to supply water through a drip irrigation system. 
For this purpose, reforestation systems with AFDS have been assigned to supply specific irriga­
tion areas. The general reforestation system with AFDS and drip irrigation network consists of

This reforestation technique or system works as follows:
Water generated on an irregular basis by means of a fog collector, is hydraulically transferred into 
a water tank where the AFDS will be installed. Once a certain water level is reached, the AFDS 
Will start running automatically and discharging water from the tank through the drip irrigation 
network and watering each plant.
Once the tank will be empty, the AFDS returns to its initial state, waiting for the tank to fill up in 
order to begin the cycle all over again.
The amount of time needed for each irrigation cycle will depend on the amount of water gener­
ated by the fog collector (or irregular source of water) and the amount of water to be stored in 
the tank before the AFDS is activated and starts irrigating and de characteristics of the irrigation 
network. For this reason, the AFDS system can be adjusted in height, so that irrigation can be 
carried out with a smaller amount of water. In other words, by regulating the AFDS, the amount 
of water needed for each irrigation is also adjusted. We suggest the regulation way because, 
depending on the results obtained from the first tests, and in order to avoid water stress in the 
plant, during low fog water collection periods, the system will allow us to shorten the irrigation 
cycles, which will translate in reducing the amount of water supplied.
Concept
AFDS is a device that allows the fluid to be dis­
charged from a tank automatically, controlled and 
autonomous, without the need for any electrical 
energy or electronic control device, joining effi­
ciency, autonomy and regularity characteristics 
without electrical consumption or CO2 emissions. 
The device is designed based on the physical 
laws of buoyancy of the Archimedes principle 
and the geometric shapes of the Gauss bell.

Location
The location of the tanks must be carefully chosen to ensure an adequate supply of water:
Tanks should be placed high up to allow water to flow by gravity, without the need for additional 
energy.
However, if they are too far from the irrigation areas, it is advisable to place tanks halfway up the 
field to adapt the water pressure, ensure a continuous flow and reduce the effort required during 
irrigation.
The location of reservoirs should be consistent with the activity being carried out and the type of 
land. In areas where irrigation is limited or where there is little vegetation, it is not necessary to 
have too big tanks. However, installing a small tank for a plot with a big need of water would not 
be very attractive either, as it would have to be constantly refilled.
It is also advantageous to be able to connect tanks in series to increase storage capacity.
The tanks must be compatible with the project’s general irrigation network, so that the stored 
water can be used efficiently. 
Amount of water
It is necessary to calculate the amount of water required during the summer so as not to run 
out, particularly for the three most important irrigations of the year. Based on the Life Nieblas 
irrigation regime, an estimate of 40 litres per plant (around 12.5L per irrigation) for the first year is 
considered. For example, for a project consisting of 3,000 plants planted over the course of the 
year, water storage of at least 120,000 litres is required. These data may vary depending on the 
project and water requirements ( part of the irrigation regime).

Irrigation frequency and quantity
Reforestation helps to limit desertification. However, after planting, the roots of young plants 
have difficulty in reaching the water in the soil, reducing their survival. Also, given the heat and 
rapid evaporation of water and the long dry summer period, watering is essential in some parts 
of the world.
Therefore you need to think about an irrigation regime that is suited to the project, taking into 
account the type of soil, rainfall and the type of tree planted. You need to think about the different 
types of irrigation possible and identify the quantity of water required and their frequency (or the 
parameter triggering irrigation).
During Life Nieblas, there were 4 types of irrigation: nursery (very regular for young plants), main­
tenance (at regular, continuous intervals and for the most difficult periods), settlement (when the 
tree is planted) and support (when the tree is under stress).

Objective
To monitor water usage within the different reforestation methods as well as the purpose of each 
irrigation event, technicians have divided water use in the following parameters: Nursery, Tradi­
tional1, AFDS1, IFWC1, Cocoon1, and IFWC2 for reforestation methods during period 2021-22. 
Purpose subdivisions are: settlement, maintenance, support, waste and other. This system al­
lows technicians to better judge the amount of water required per reforestation system but also 
to find out about possible losses, incidents and other activities that require the use of water.
Material
For traditional hose irrigation, the amount of water present before and after irrigation should be 
noted, so that the amount of water used can be calculated by subtraction.
In addition, to find out how many plants have been watered, the use of a tally counter is very 
useful.
For each AFDS reforestation system, water metres with pulse sensors will be installed, as well 
as communication via Wi-Fi, with the data collection system located in the meteorological tower. 
This is to determine the following:
	
࡟The number of discharges/irrigations performed in the system.
	
࡟The frequency of these irrigations.
	
࡟The quantity or volume of water (in m3) supplied in each irrigation.
